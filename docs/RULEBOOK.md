# Burmese Poker — The Rulebook

*The game, taught front to back for somebody who has never seen it. Derived from the official
rules in `RULES.md` at **rev 30** — that document decides every rule; this one only teaches
them. A short list of points our table has had to choose for itself is at the end.*

---

## What this is

A rummy played for money. Everyone is dealt thirteen cards and races to arrange all thirteen
into runs and sets by drawing and discarding one card a turn. Nothing is ever played to the
table along the way — you build the whole hand in secret and reveal it all at once when you
win.

On top of the race sits the thing that makes this game *this* game: certain cards are **money
cards**, and they pay their owner at the end of every round no matter who won. You can lose
the round and still leave the table richer.

If you know any rummy — Indian Rummy especially — the skeleton will feel familiar. The money
layer will not. It has no parallel in any published game, and it is taught in full below.

## What you need

- **Two standard decks shuffled together, jokers in** — 52 + 52 + 4 jokers = **108 cards**.
- **Two to six players.** Five is the usual table. (Past six the draw pile runs too thin to
  play; if you must seat seven or more, add a third deck.)
- **Two stakes, agreed before play and held all session:**

  | Stake | Typical | What it is |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Round value** | $5 | What a loser pays the winner when a round ends. |
  | **Money card value** | $1 | What each money card pays its owner, per opponent. |

- Equal banks for everyone. Money is the only score the game keeps — there are no points.

A session is just rounds played one after another with the banks carrying over. Nothing ends
it; you stop when you decide to stop.

## Setting up

1. Shuffle both decks together.
2. **Draw for seats.** Seating is random, and it is drawn **once** — you keep your seat round
   after round until the whole table agrees to draw again. Turn order is fixed and circular.
3. **Deal thirteen cards** to each player, one at a time around the table.
4. **Turn up two cards** — one from the **bottom** of the deck, one from the **top** — and lay
   them face up on the table. These two designate the round's money cards (explained in *The
   money cards*, below). They stay on the table all round; the rest of the deck becomes the
   draw pile.

Because the money cards are turned up **after** the deal, you may find you are already holding
a copy of a card that has just become money. That is the point.

One rare thing can happen before anybody moves: a player's dealt thirteen may already be a
winning hand. If it is, they may declare it immediately — see *Winning on the deal* under
*What a win pays*.

## A turn

On your turn:

1. **Take one card** — either the card the previous player just discarded, or the top of the
   draw pile, blind.
2. **Throw one card away**, face up onto your own discard pile.

That is the whole turn. You hold thirteen cards between turns and fourteen during your own.

Details that matter:

- Only the **immediately previous** player's most recent discard is ever available to you.
  You cannot take from across the table, and you cannot dig into a pile.
- **Each player keeps their own discard pile**, face up in front of them — and the piles are
  **public**. Anyone may pick one up and read through it at any time.
- **You may throw back the very card you just took**, as long as no other rule stops you —
  in practice that means the feeding rule, next.
- **If the draw pile runs out**, gather every discard pile, shuffle them together, and that
  becomes the new draw pile. The two turned-up cards stay where they are. (Rounds usually end
  long before this happens.)

## The one restriction on what you may throw

Everything in your hand is yours to throw — with a single exception, which is really a rule of
table manners: **you may not feed the player after you.**

When the player after you takes a card **in the open** — your discard, or the turned-up money
card on the opening turn — everyone has seen what they are collecting. From that moment, **you
may not discard any card of that rank** for the rest of the round. Rank only: watch them take
a Q♦ and every Queen in your hand is closed, whatever its suit. Each rank they take in the open
closes independently, and the closures last the round. Nothing carries into the next round.

Only you are bound — you are the one seat whose discards they can take, so you are the only
one who could feed them. And a **blind draw closes nothing**: nobody saw what it was.

Two ways a closed rank opens again:

1. **They throw one back.** If the protected player ever discards a card of that rank
   themselves, that rank opens **for the rest of the round, permanently** — even if they later
   pick another one up. Throwing a Queen away is a public statement that they are not
   collecting Queens, and it is not retracted.
2. **You are going out on it.** The throw that wins you the round (see *Winning*) may be any
   card in your hand, closed rank or not. The round ends on it, so the gift is never delivered.

There is no penalty and nothing to argue about, because throwing a closed card is not an
illegal move — it is **not a move at all**. At the table, the other players simply do not let
the card land; you pick it back up and throw something else.

And the rule never traps you: if every card in your hand is somehow of a closed rank — it takes
a strange hand, but two ranks can do it — the restriction **yields for that turn** and you may
throw anything. You must always discard, so you always can.

*(Jokers count as a rank of their own here: watch the player after you take a joker in the
open, and all four jokers are closed to you.)*

## The opening turn's extra option

The first player of the round has a choice nobody else gets: instead of drawing, they may
**take the turned-up top money card** off the table — the actual card, which leaves the table
for good.

But they must **ask permission first — from the player who plays before them** in turn order
(the last seat around the table, the one whose discards they will be taking all round). That
player may refuse **only if they are holding a card of that rank** — because the claim is a
take in the open, so it would close that rank to them under the feeding rule, locking a card
into their hand for the round. The veto is their way out.

Two things follow, and both are worth knowing before you ever claim or refuse:

- **Refusing shows your hand.** Only a holder may say no, so an objection tells the whole
  table you hold that rank. It is the only thing in this game a player reveals by choice.
- **A claimed card never pays you money.** As you will see under *The money cards*, only cards
  the deck gives you pay. The claim buys melding value, nothing else.

If the claim is refused, the card stays on the table and the opener draws blind as usual — and
a refused claim closes nothing, since nothing was taken.

## The melds

A winning hand is thirteen cards arranged entirely into **runs** and **sets**.

**A run (series)** — three or more cards of the **same suit** in **consecutive rank**.
Aces are high or low, never both: `A-2-3` is a run, `Q-K-A` is a run, `K-A-2` is not.

**A set** — three or more cards of the **same rank**, **all suits different**. Two decks mean
you can hold `9♥ 9♥ 9♠`, but it is not a set — duplicate suits are forbidden, so a set never
has more than four cards.

**Jokers** stand in for any missing card, in a run or a set, with no limit on how many one
meld can carry — three jokers laid down together are a legal run. But a meld with a joker in
it is never a **clean** one, and clean runs matter to the win condition below.

**Nothing is ever laid on the table during play.** No melding in front of you, no laying off
on anyone else's cards. Your hand stays hidden until the moment you declare it, and the only
public information in the game is the discard piles.

## Winning

You win by **declaring**: on your turn, take your card as usual — now holding fourteen —
**throw one away, then lay down all thirteen** arranged into valid melds. The discard always
comes first, and it is never optional. There is no way to go out without throwing a card.

**What the thirteen must contain depends on how many of you are playing** — and the runs a
table size *requires* must be **clean** (joker-free):

| Players | The declared hand must contain | …of which clean |
|---:|---|---|
| **2** | **Runs only — sets are not allowed at all** | no requirement |
| **3** | at least **two runs** | **both of them** |
| **4** | at least **one run** | **that one** |
| **5 or 6** | anything — no requirement | no requirement |

The fewer the players, the more of the deck each of you draws through, so the hand is made
harder to compensate. A long run may be counted as more than one: declare a six-card run as
`3+3` and it is two runs. And an all-joker run can never be one of the required clean ones.

Declaring is always a choice. If your thirteen already meld and you would rather play on —
perhaps to go out clean — you may.

## What a win pays

Settlement happens in two independent steps, in this order. The first is about the win; the
second — the money cards — settles regardless of who won and is taught in the next chapter.

**Step 1 — the round payment.** Every other player pays the winner the **round value**. Flat:
$5 a head at typical stakes, whether they were one card short or holding all thirteen unmelded.
There is no counting of what the losers held, ever.

Three things can change that payment:

- **Going out jokerless.** No joker anywhere in the declared thirteen — sets and runs alike —
  and the round payment is multiplied: **×2** with two, three or four players, **×3** with
  five or more. This is a property of the cards you laid down, not of which runs you needed.
  It is the reason a joker is ever thrown away.
- **Winning on the deal.** If the thirteen you were *dealt* already meld, you may declare
  before anyone draws a card, and the round payment is **×2**. The offer goes around in turn
  order before the first take; you may also decline and play on. The two multipliers stack:
  dealt a jokerless winner at a five-player table, you are paid ×2 × ×3 = **×6**.
- **A third win in a row.** Win three consecutive rounds and the **player immediately before
  you in turn order pays your whole round payment alone** — everyone else pays nothing. They
  are blamed for feeding you: theirs are the discards you have been taking. You still collect
  exactly what you would have collected; it just all comes out of one pocket. Keep winning and
  it keeps working this way, round after round, until you lose one.

All three touch the round payment only. The money cards, next, settle the same way whatever
the round was worth.

**Step 2 — the money cards** settle pairwise, owner by owner — the whole next chapter.

## The money cards

This is the layer that makes the game. Certain cards pay their **owner** the money card value
from **every other player** at the end of the round — win or lose.

**Which cards are money:**

1. **Always: the 7♦, the A♠, and every joker** — all copies, both decks. Eight cards, every
   round, by tradition.
2. **This round: the two turned-up cards, and their exact twins.** Turn up a 5♥ and both 5♥s
   are money this round. Exact match only — rank *and* suit; the other fives are nothing. (A
   turned-up joker's twin is the other joker of the **same colour**.)

**Ownership is everything, and it works like this:**

- **You own a money card only if the deck gave it to you** — dealt in your thirteen, or drawn
  blind from the pile. A card taken from a discard, or claimed off the table, is held but
  **never owned**.
- **Ownership is permanent.** Once the deck gives you a money card, it pays you at settlement
  — even if you discard it, even if an opponent is holding it when the round ends. Nobody can
  take a money card away from you, and you never need to keep one.

So throw money cards away freely. Play your hand to win; the money was settled the moment the
deck moved.

- **The two turned-up cards themselves are owned by nobody**, claimed or not. They designate;
  they never pay.

**What each pays.** At settlement, each owned money card pays its owner the money card value
— times its multiplier — from every other player:

| The card | Pays its owner |
|---|---:|
| An ordinary money card (a turn-up's twin, or a permanent one) | **×1** |
| The twin of a turned-up 7♦, A♠ or joker | **×3** |
| The 7♦ and A♠ turned up **together**, one player owning **both** twins | **×5 each** |

The ×3 is what happens when the turn-up lands on a card that was already money: the shown copy
can no longer pay anyone, so its value moves to the twin. The ×5 is the jackpot — the only
place in the game where what a card pays depends on who holds what. At typical stakes at a
five-player table it is $40, against a $5 round prize.

Everyone settles with everyone — the winner pays and collects in this step like anybody else.

## Changing seats

Seats hold from round to round. Any player may propose drawing seats again in the gap between
rounds; the seats are re-drawn **when the table agrees to it**, and only then.

## A worked round

A real round, dealt and played by the computer rather than invented — seed **15**, five
players, typical stakes ($5 round / $1 money card). The seats in turn order: **Aye**, **Bo**,
**Cho**, **Da**, **Ei**. (Every card and every dollar below is exactly what the engine dealt
and settled at that seed; a test replays it.)

**The deal.** Thirteen each:

| Seat | Dealt |
|---|---|
| **Aye** | 8♥ 8♠ 6♦ 🃏Red 10♥ 2♦ 8♣ K♥ 8♠ 8♦ 6♥ 4♥ 3♦ |
| **Bo** | K♠ 4♠ 4♥ 7♣ A♥ 3♦ 10♥ 3♠ 9♦ 7♥ K♠ 9♠ 3♥ |
| **Cho** | 4♣ K♦ J♦ Q♥ 2♠ 🃏Red 5♥ A♠ J♥ J♣ 3♣ 6♠ 9♠ |
| **Da** | 4♣ K♣ A♣ A♠ 9♦ 5♣ 2♥ 10♦ 3♣ 6♣ 5♥ 9♣ 2♦ |
| **Ei** | A♦ 10♣ 7♥ 5♣ 2♠ 4♦ 8♦ 6♠ 5♦ 9♥ Q♥ 7♦ J♠ |

**The turn-up:** the **2♣** from the bottom of the deck and the **6♥** from the top. So this
round's money, on top of the permanent eight, is the other 2♣ and the other 6♥ — and Aye is
*already holding* that 6♥, dealt seconds before it became money. The two shown cards lie on
the table and pay nobody.

**What the deck gave out.** Seven money cards found owners — six in the deal, and the
seventh, Aye's 2♣, drawn blind during play:

| The card | The deck gave it to | It pays them |
|---|---|---|
| 2♣ (×1) | Aye | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| red joker (×1) | Aye | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| 6♥ (×1) | Aye | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| A♠ (×1) | Cho | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| red joker (×1) | Cho | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| A♠ (×1) | Da | $1 × 4 = **$4** |
| 7♦ (×1) | Ei | $1 × 4 = **$4** |

(The two black jokers and the second 7♦ found no owner and pay nobody. Nothing was turned up
on a permanent money card, so nothing pays ×3 this time.)

**The race.** The round ran **24 turns** before **Da** went out, declaring:

> 2♥ 2♠ 2♦ &nbsp;·&nbsp; 3♣ 4♣ 5♣ 6♣ &nbsp;·&nbsp; Q♣ K♣ A♣ &nbsp;·&nbsp; 9♠ 9♣ 9♦

Two sets and two runs — legal at five players, where nothing particular is required — and
**not a joker anywhere in the thirteen**, so the round payment is **×3**: each loser pays
$5 × 3 = $15, and Da collects $60. Notice what Da laid down: the A♠ they were dealt is not in
it. Somewhere in those 24 turns Da threw an owned money card away — and is still paid for it,
because the deck gave it to them and ownership never moves.

**The settlement, in full.** Round payment first, then every money card, owner by owner —
each player pays $1 per card everybody *else* owns, and collects for their own:

| Seat | Round payment | Money cards | Net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Aye | -$15 | +$8 | **-$7** |
| Bo | -$15 | -$7 | **-$22** |
| Cho | -$15 | +$3 | **-$12** |
| Da | +$60 | -$2 | **+$58** |
| Ei | -$15 | -$2 | **-$17** |

Reading the money column: Aye owns three cards (collects $12) and pays $1 apiece for the four
owned by others (−$4), net +$8. Bo owns nothing and pays for all seven: −$7. Da, the winner,
owns one and pays for six: collects $4, pays $6, net −$2 — the winner settles the money like
anybody else. Every column sums to zero; the money moved, it did not appear.

## The table reference

Keep this page out on the table.

**A turn:** take one — the previous player's discard, or blind off the pile — then throw one.

**You may not throw:** any rank the player after you has taken in the open, until they throw
that rank back (opens it for good) or your throw is the one you go out on. A blind draw closes
nothing.

**Opening turn only:** the first player may take the turned-up top money card instead of
drawing — with the permission of the seat before them, who may refuse only if they hold that
rank.

**The melds:** runs — 3+, one suit, consecutive, aces never wrap. Sets — 3+, one rank, all
suits different, four cards at most. Jokers stand in anywhere, but a meld with one is not
clean.

**To win:** take, **throw**, then lay down all thirteen melded. Required runs by table size:
2 players — runs only, no sets; 3 — two runs, both clean; 4 — one run, clean; 5 or 6 — no
requirement.

**The winner collects** the round value from everyone — **×2** jokerless (2–4 players) or
**×3** (5–6), **×2** again dealt-complete, multipliers stacking; a **third straight win** is
paid entirely by the seat before the winner.

**The money cards** — the 7♦, A♠ and all jokers always; the two turn-ups' exact twins this
round. Owned only if the deck dealt or drew them to you, and owned for ever. Each pays its
owner ×1 the money value per opponent; a turned-up permanent's twin pays **×3**; the 7♦/A♠
pair to one owner pays **×5 each**. Turned-up cards pay nobody.

## How our table reads the close calls

Every rule above is the game as it is played. A handful of situations come up rarely enough
that no two tables are guaranteed to agree, and we had to pick a reading. If you learned the
game elsewhere, these are the lines to compare before money is on the table:

1. **Shedding a joker early** (#33). Going out clean sometimes means throwing a joker away
   while jokers are closed against you. We allow it only as the winning discard — the ordinary
   going-out exception — never a turn earlier.
2. **The jokerless bonus and the money cards** (#36). The ×2/×3 multiplies the round payment
   only. Money cards settle exactly as always.
3. **Six players** (#37). Jokerless pays ×3 at six, the same as five.
4. **What "winning on the deal" means** (#38). The thirteen you were dealt, before anyone
   draws. A hand completed on your first turn is just a fast ordinary win.
5. **Stacking the bonuses** (#39). The deal ×2 and the jokerless ×2/×3 multiply together.
6. **The deal bonus and the money cards** (#40). Round payment only, like the other bonus.
7. **A fourth win in a row** (#41). The streak keeps firing — the seat before the winner goes
   on paying alone until the streak breaks.
8. **The streak and the money cards** (#44). The substitution covers the round payment only;
   money cards settle pairwise as always.
9. **Changing seats** (#45). Seats are re-drawn when the players agree to it — not because one
   player demands it.
10. **What "agree" means** (#47). Everybody. One objection keeps the seats where they are.
11. **A re-seat in the middle of a streak** (#46). Whoever sits immediately before the winner
    in the round they win their third — that seating, not an earlier one — pays.
12. **Two players dealt winning hands at once** (#48). The earlier in turn order wins. The
    offer goes around in turn order, and the first to accept takes the round.

The numbers are the rows in `RULES.md` §9 where each reading is recorded, with the reasoning
and the test that will change the day one of them is answered for good.
